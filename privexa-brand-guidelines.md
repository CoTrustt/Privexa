# Privexa Brand & Product Design Guidelines

## 1. Brand thesis

**Privexa should feel like the professional privacy operating system a DPO would trust with consequential work.**

The experience should combine:

- the restraint and craft associated with premium Apple software;
- the confidence and evidentiary discipline expected in a professional privacy practice;
- the minimalism of a focused productivity tool;
- the transparency required for AI-assisted regulatory work; and
- the calm of a system designed to reduce judgement load rather than create more dashboards.

The intended emotional response is:

> **Calm, private, precise, defensible, and under human control.**

Privexa is not a cybersecurity console, a generic GRC database, a legal research portal, or an AI chatbot with privacy features. The brand should communicate a **professional operating environment for privacy judgement**.

---

# 2. Product context the brand must support

Privexa is built around a common privacy intelligence layer:

**Client Privacy Memory → Processing Activities → RoPA → Evidence → Questions / DPIAs → DPO Opinion → Decision → Actions → Evidence → updated Client Memory / RoPA**

The visual system must therefore support three simultaneous modes of work:

1. **Operating** — maintaining RoPA, evidence, actions, requests, client context.
2. **Reasoning** — preparing Opinions, DPIAs, recommendations, gaps and regulatory analysis.
3. **Judging** — making human decisions, accepting residual risk, approving changes and preserving an auditable record.

The interface must make these modes feel like parts of one coherent product rather than separate compliance modules.

---

# 3. Brand idea: Quiet Authority

## 3.1 Brand essence

**Quiet Authority** is the core design idea.

Privexa should never need to look “secure” by using dark hacker aesthetics, shields, glowing gradients, dense risk dashboards or alarming red indicators. Trust should come from the quality of the interaction:

- clear provenance;
- stable information architecture;
- precise language;
- obvious client boundaries;
- calm review states;
- visible evidence;
- reversible AI suggestions;
- intentional confirmation around consequential actions; and
- a strong separation between machine preparation and professional judgement.

## 3.2 Brand attributes

| Attribute | Meaning in the product |
|---|---|
| **Private** | Client boundaries, restricted context and source usage are understandable without creating security theatre. |
| **Precise** | Language, spacing, hierarchy and data presentation are deliberate. |
| **Calm** | The product reduces visual and cognitive noise. |
| **Professional** | It feels appropriate for DPOs, privacy consultants, legal teams and executive stakeholders. |
| **Evidence-led** | Claims and recommendations are connected to evidence, law and provenance. |
| **Human-controlled** | AI prepares; people review, decide, approve and sign. |
| **Continuous** | RoPA, evidence and client memory feel living rather than annual/static. |
| **Premium** | Craft is visible in typography, motion, spacing, materials and interaction quality rather than decoration. |

## 3.3 Brand promise

**Privexa turns privacy knowledge into defensible professional action.**

## 3.4 Optional positioning lines

Preferred:

> **Privacy intelligence, under professional control.**

Alternatives:

- **The operating system for modern privacy practice.**
- **From processing facts to defensible decisions.**
- **Know the client. Understand the risk. Make the decision.**

Avoid slogans that imply autonomous legal judgement, such as “Your AI DPO”, “Automatic compliance”, or “Never worry about privacy again”.

# 4. Apple influence: principles, not imitation

The product may be heavily influenced by how Apple approaches digital product design, but it should not reproduce Apple visual assets, branded components, marketing layouts or proprietary iconography.

Use the following principles instead:

1. **Content first** — the work is the visual centre of gravity.
2. **Progressive disclosure** — show the minimum required for the current decision.
3. **Strong typographic hierarchy** — use type before borders, containers or color.
4. **Generous negative space** — make complex regulatory work feel manageable.
5. **Direct manipulation** — edit the object in place rather than sending users through setup wizards.
6. **Subtle depth** — use elevation only to establish hierarchy.
7. **Predictability** — controls behave consistently everywhere.
8. **Purposeful motion** — animation explains state change; it never performs.
9. **High-quality defaults** — users should rarely need to configure presentation.
10. **Quiet confidence** — important screens should feel composed, not “dashboarded”.

A useful test:

> If the screen can lose one label, border, card, icon or color without losing meaning, remove it.

# 5. Primary audiences

## 5.1 Privacy consultant / fractional DPO

Needs:

- fast client switching;
- strong context retention;
- minimal administrative burden;
- confidence in evidence and AI provenance;
- rapid movement between RoPA, DPIA, Opinion, Decisions and Actions;
- professional outputs suitable for client use.

The interface should feel like a **workbench**, not an admin portal.

## 5.2 In-house DPO / privacy leader

Needs:

- confidence in current processing;
- visibility into material issues;
- defensible judgement;
- clear action ownership;
- concise executive communication.

The interface should feel like a **calm control surface**, not a risk-monitoring wall.

## 5.3 Client stakeholder

Needs:

- ordinary-language requests;
- minimal privacy jargon;
- clear explanation of what is required;
- confidence that uploaded information is being handled appropriately.

The portal should feel like a **secure, simple collaboration space**.

## 5.4 Firm partner / executive

Needs:

- capacity;
- judgement load;
- meaningful client changes;
- commercial leverage;
- defensible executive summaries.

The product should compress complexity rather than expose internal machinery.

# 6. Core design principles

## 6.1 Human judgement is visually sovereign

The strongest visual boundary in Privexa is not between modules. It is between:

**Privexa prepares** and **the professional decides**.

AI-generated or AI-prepared content must never visually masquerade as an approved conclusion.

Use explicit state language:

- **Prepared by Privexa**
- **Suggested update**
- **Needs review**
- **Verified**
- **Approved by [person]**
- **Your Decision**

Never preselect a consequential decision.

---

## 6.2 Privacy is visible, not theatrical

Show privacy context where it changes user understanding:

- persistent active client/tenant;
- “Restricted client context” for sensitive AI operations;
- context/source disclosure;
- clear cross-client confirmation;
- explicit external sharing state;
- obvious client/firm knowledge boundaries.

Avoid:

- a lock icon on every page;
- “military-grade” claims;
- neon security colors;
- giant compliance badges;
- permanent warning banners for normal secure behavior.

---

## 6.3 Evidence outranks decoration

Whenever a recommendation or AI analysis matters, make its basis easier to inspect.

Prefer:

**Finding → why it matters → source / evidence → action**

over:

**score → chart → color → tooltip**

Avoid synthetic “health scores” unless there is a rigorous deterministic method and a real user decision attached to the score.

---

## 6.4 Reduce judgement load

A DPO should not have to scan 40 widgets to understand what matters.

The interface should repeatedly answer:

- What changed?
- What requires my judgement?
- What is Privexa handling?
- What evidence supports this?
- What is missing?
- What happens next?

---

## 6.5 One privacy memory, multiple professional views

RoPA, DPIA, Opinions, Evidence and Actions should feel connected.

Relationships should be present in the UI through:

- source chips;
- related-object counts;
- inspector panels;
- “affected processing activities”;
- “used in this decision”;
- “open actions”;
- “evidence supporting this”.

Avoid separate visual identities for every module.

# 7. Visual identity direction

## 7.1 Overall visual character

Use a **light, neutral, high-clarity base** with one confident Privexa accent color.

The default product should feel closer to:

- premium operating system;
- professional document editor;
- modern enterprise productivity tool;

and farther from:

- SOC dashboard;
- crypto application;
- generic SaaS admin template;
- legal case-management software from the 2000s;
- colorful startup analytics dashboard.

## 7.2 Material language

Privexa surfaces should use:

- mostly white or near-white backgrounds;
- thin neutral separators;
- soft radii;
- extremely restrained shadows;
- muted fills for grouping;
- occasional translucent/blurred surfaces only when hierarchy benefits.

Do not use glassmorphism as a visual theme. Blur is a functional material treatment, not decoration.

# 9. Typography

## 9.1 Typeface strategy

Use the native system UI stack wherever possible:

```css
font-family:
 
  "Space Grotesk", headings and body
 
  Space Mono, labels
 
```

This creates a native, premium feel on Apple devices while remaining excellent across Windows and Linux.

Do not package or redistribute Apple font files.

For long-form regulatory/editor content, retain the same sans-serif family by default. Privexa should feel like a modern professional workspace rather than a traditional law journal.

## 9.2 Type scale

| Role | Size / line height | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Display | `32 / 40` | 600 |
| Page title | `28 / 36` | 600 |
| Section title | `20 / 28` | 600 |
| Card title | `16 / 24` | 600 |
| Body | `15 / 22` | 400 |
| Body strong | `15 / 22` | 500–600 |
| Small | `13 / 18` | 400–500 |
| Micro / label | `12 / 16` | 500–600 |

Use weight sparingly. Privexa should never look bold everywhere.

## 9.3 Typography rules

- Use sentence case for almost all UI copy.
- Reserve uppercase for short semantic knowledge labels such as `LAW`.
- Avoid all-caps navigation.
- Prefer numbers aligned with tabular numerals in dense tables.
- Keep line lengths around 60–80 characters in long-form reasoning.
- Use paragraph spacing rather than excessive card separation.

# 10. Spacing and layout

## 10.1 Base spacing scale

Use a 4 px primitive with an 8 px primary rhythm.

```text
4   micro
8   tight
12  compact
16  standard
20  standard+
24  section
32  major
40  large
48  large+
64  page
80  marketing/hero only
```

The product should feel spacious but not wasteful. Density may increase in tables and inspectors, not in decision surfaces.

## 10.2 Application shell

Recommended desktop structure:

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Top bar / active tenant / global controls                   │
├──────────────┬──────────────────────────────┬───────────────┤
│ Global nav   │ Main work surface            │ Inspector     │
│ 220–240 px   │ flexible                     │ 320–380 px    │
└──────────────┴──────────────────────────────┴───────────────┘
```

The right inspector is contextual, not permanently open.

Use it for:

- relationships;
- evidence;
- affected RoPA activities;
- source provenance;
- metadata;
- AI context;
- object history.

## 10.3 Content widths

- Dashboard/list content: max `1200–1280 px`
- Reading/editor canvas: `720–860 px`
- Structured DPIA workspace: `900–1100 px`
- Inspector: `320–380 px`
- Modal: prefer sheets or focused dialogs; avoid very large generic modals.

---

# 11. Shape, depth and borders

## 11.1 Radius

| Element | Radius |
|---|---:|
| Small controls | `8 px` |
| Standard controls | `10 px` |
| Cards / panels | `12 px` |
| Sheets / large containers | `16 px` |
| Pills | full |

Avoid excessive 20–28 px “bubbly SaaS” radii.

## 11.2 Shadows

Use shadows only where an object is physically elevated in the interaction model:

- command menu;
- dropdown;
- popover;
- modal;
- floating inspector;
- drag state.

Static cards should generally rely on spacing + border + tonal separation.

---

# 12. Iconography

Use a simple line-icon family consistent with the existing shadcn/Radix stack, e.g. **Lucide**.

Guidelines:

- 16 px inside compact controls;
- 18–20 px in navigation;
- 1.5–2 px optical stroke;
- labels accompany ambiguous icons;
- use filled icons only for rare selected-state emphasis;
- do not use a shield as the generic icon for everything privacy-related.

Good conceptual motifs:

- evidence: document / attachment;
- processing activity: connected nodes / flow;
- decision: check-circle / scale abstraction;
- action: check list;
- privacy memory: layered records;
- restricted context: lock;
- AI assistance: subtle sparkle only where assistance is actionable.

The sparkle icon should not become a permanent AI watermark across the product.

---

# 13. Logo and brand mark direction

The Privexa identity should be simple enough to work at favicon scale.

## Preferred concept territory

### A. Protected aperture

A geometric mark built from two opposing forms that create a protected central negative space.

Represents:

- privacy boundary;
- focused judgement;
- controlled disclosure;
- protected client context.

### B. Privacy lens

A circular/rounded-square mark with a precise inset or aperture.

Represents:

- seeing what matters;
- understanding context;
- controlled visibility.

### C. Structured “P”

A monoline or constructed `P` with an internal boundary/gap rather than a literal shield.

Represents:

- Privexa;
- protected knowledge;
- professional systems thinking.

## Avoid

- padlocks as the primary logo;
- generic shields;
- fingerprints;
- hooded/hacker imagery;
- circuit-brain AI motifs;
- legal scales;
- GDPR stars;
- gradients as the defining brand asset.

The logo should normally appear monochrome in the application shell. Use accent color selectively in marketing or onboarding.

# 14. Navigation architecture

## 14.1 Global navigation

Keep global navigation intentionally limited:

- **Home**
- **Clients**
- **Ask Privexa**
- **Firm Brain**
- **Intelligence**
- **Settings**

The global shell should communicate practice-level context.

## 14.2 Client navigation

Within a client:

- **Overview**
- **RoPA**
- **DPIAs**
- **Evidence**
- **Decisions**
- **Actions**

Do not create separate top-level navigation for every backend domain object.

Questions, obligations, history, client memory and AI context can be exposed contextually.

## 14.3 Active client boundary

The active client name should remain visible in the top bar or client header.

Example:

> **Apollo Finance**

This is an important privacy affordance, not decoration.

Cross-client movement involving data, evidence or AI context should require an explicit transition/confirmation when appropriate.

---

# 15. Screen grammar

Every major Privexa screen should use a consistent visual grammar:

```text
Page title
Short state/context line
Primary action

[Main work surface]

Contextual issues / review items
Source-backed detail
Relationships
History / provenance when requested
```

Avoid arbitrary dashboard card grids.

Use cards only when they represent genuine objects or distinct work units.

---

# 16. Buttons and actions

## 16.1 Button hierarchy

### Primary
One clear forward action per work surface.

Examples:

- **Add activity**
- **Review changes**
- **Create DPIA**
- **Use in Opinion**
- **Approve with conditions**

### Secondary
Low-emphasis bordered or tonal.

Examples:

- **Discover from evidence**
- **Ask client**
- **View sources**
- **Export**

### Tertiary
Text/icon action for low-stakes interaction.

Examples:

- **Edit**
- **Copy**
- **Show history**

### Destructive
Explicit and isolated.

Examples:

- **Delete evidence**
- **Remove client**
- **Revoke access**

Never use two visually equal primary actions adjacent to each other when the decision has different consequences.

---

# 17. Forms

DPO work involves structured data, but Privexa should not feel like an endless regulatory form system.

Rules:

- show only fields relevant to the current object/state;
- use natural grouping and progressive disclosure;
- preserve user-entered data when switching tabs or panels;
- allow AI to prefill, but mark material AI-prepared content until reviewed;
- show source references beside prefilled facts;
- use inline validation;
- avoid asterisks everywhere—state optional fields when that is the exception;
- support keyboard-first data entry in RoPA table mode.

Use `React Hook Form + Zod + JSON Schema` for dynamic workflows while keeping the visual system consistent.

---

# 18. Tables and registers

RoPA and portfolio views will require dense information.

Tables should still feel premium.

## Table rules

- 44–48 px standard row height;
- 36–40 px compact review mode;
- sticky header where helpful;
- strong first column;
- muted secondary metadata;
- minimal vertical borders;
- row hover only when the row is interactive;
- visible sort/filter state;
- optional columns rather than horizontal overload;
- quick keyboard navigation for power users.

Use table mode as a professional alternative, not the only RoPA experience.

---

# 19. RoPA experience

## 19.1 Brand objective

RoPA should feel like the **living processing map of the client**, not a spreadsheet recreated on the web.

## 19.2 Default screen

Example:

```text
Apollo Finance — RoPA

27 processing activities
3 need review · 2 potentially changed since your last review

[Add activity]   Discover from evidence
```

Then a clear list:

| Processing activity | Purpose | Data principals | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer onboarding | Open customer account | Customers | Current |
| Fraud monitoring | Detect suspicious activity | Customers | Review |
| Employee payroll | Pay employees | Employees | Current |
| Support analytics | Improve support performance | Customers | Missing retention |

## 19.3 Activity detail

Use spacious sections:

- Purpose
- Personal data
- Data principals
- Processors
- Retention
- Geography
- Safeguards
- Obligations
- Evidence
- Related decisions
- Open actions

AI findings appear as a small review section:

**Privexa found**

> The current privacy notice describes customer support but does not clearly describe analytics reuse.

Then:

**Review processing activity**

Do not lead with:

- completion percentages;
- red/amber/green maturity dials;
- generic risk scores;
- 20-field grids.

## 19.4 Continuous RoPA

Suggested updates must be separate from authoritative data.

Use a diff:

```text
Current                           Proposed
Vendor A                          Vendor A
—                                 Vendor B
Retention: Not documented        Retention: 24 months
```

Actions:

- **Accept update**
- **Edit**
- **Reject**

Privexa must never silently rewrite the official RoPA.

# 20. DPIA experience

## 20.1 Brand objective

A DPIA should feel like **structured professional reasoning over facts Privexa already knows**.

It must not begin as a 60-question blank form.

## 20.2 Header

```text
AI Customer Support Training
DPIA · Draft

Linked to 2 RoPA processing activities
```

## 20.3 First section: What we already know

Show source-backed known facts:

- Purpose
- Data categories
- Data principals
- Processors
- Geography
- Existing safeguards

Every imported fact can expose its source.

## 20.4 Second section: What still requires assessment

Use numbered material questions:

1. Is the processing necessary for the stated purpose?
2. Could a less intrusive approach achieve the objective?
3. What privacy harms could arise?
4. Are current safeguards sufficient?
5. What residual risk remains?

AI analysis should appear beneath the question, with:

- **Prepared by Privexa**
- sources;
- assumptions;
- gaps;
- editable reasoning.

## 20.5 Risk scenarios

Avoid abstract matrix-first presentation.

Prefer:

```text
What could happen
Who could be affected
Why this could occur
Existing control
Residual concern
Additional measure
```

A risk matrix can be available secondarily where the firm requires it.

## 20.6 Decision surface

The final section must visually reset the page:

# Your DPIA Decision

- **Proceed**
- **Proceed with additional measures**
- **Do not proceed**
- **Need more information**

No option is preselected.

The design principle is:

> **Privexa prepares the DPIA. The DPO owns the conclusion.**

# 21. DPO Opinion experience

The Opinion workspace should feel closer to a premium writing environment than a form.

Suggested structure:

- Facts
- Missing facts
- Applicable law
- Firm position
- Analysis
- Proposed position
- Conditions
- Final decision

Use a centered editor with a contextual right inspector for:

- affected RoPA activities;
- client facts;
- evidence;
- obligations;
- prior decisions;
- AI context.

AI actions are contextual:

- **Prepare with Privexa**
- **Find supporting evidence**
- **Check assumptions**
- **Identify affected processing**

Avoid a permanent chat panel next to the document.

# 22. AI interaction model

## 22.1 AI is a capability, not a visual theme

Privexa is AI-native, but the interface should not look like an “AI app”.

Do not decorate the product with:

- gradients;
- glowing borders;
- chatbot avatars;
- animated sparkles;
- purple AI panels;
- “magic” terminology.

AI should appear exactly where it contributes to professional work.

## 22.2 AI states

Use a small consistent state vocabulary:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Prepared by Privexa** | AI generated or materially composed this content. |
| **Suggested** | AI has proposed a change, link or action. |
| **Needs review** | Material human validation is required. |
| **Verified** | A professional has accepted/confirmed the content. |
| **Source-backed** | The statement has inspectable evidence/context. |
| **Assumption** | A fact is not established and has been intentionally recorded as an assumption. |

Once an accepted extracted fact becomes ordinary Client Memory, remove unnecessary AI decoration.

## 22.3 Context disclosure

For material AI output, make **Context used** expandable.

Group by:

- Client facts
- RoPA
- Evidence
- Previous Decisions
- Regulatory sources
- Firm positions

Do not expose raw retrieval/debug information by default.

## 22.4 Provenance

Detailed provenance belongs in an expandable professional layer:

- source objects;
- model class/provider policy where appropriate;
- task type;
- time;
- output version;
- human acceptance/edit state.

The normal user experience should remain clean.

## 22.5 Restricted context

For sensitive operations, a subtle treatment is sufficient:

`Restricted client context`

Use a small lock icon + muted label. Avoid warning-color styling unless there is an actual issue.

---

# 23. Knowledge-type visual grammar

Privexa needs an explicit distinction between different kinds of propositions.

Use restrained semantic labels:

- `LAW`
- `FIRM POSITION`
- `CLIENT FACT`
- `ASSUMPTION`
- `RECOMMENDATION`

Recommended treatment:

```text
LAW
DPDP Act · Section / rule reference
Text / summary...

CLIENT FACT
Customer support conversations are retained for 24 months.
Source: Support Architecture · reviewed 18 Aug 2026
```

Labels should be small, neutral and consistent. Do not assign a loud unique color to every category.

Possible restrained differentiation:

- LAW — indigo-gray
- FIRM POSITION — slate
- CLIENT FACT — neutral
- ASSUMPTION — amber
- RECOMMENDATION — accent

Only `ASSUMPTION` needs heightened visual attention because uncertainty is material.

---

# 24. Evidence experience

Evidence should transition from **file** to **meaningful professional object**.

An evidence detail view should answer:

- What is this?
- What does it establish?
- What does it contradict?
- How fresh is it?
- Which processing activities depend on it?
- Which decisions/DPIAs use it?
- Has a professional verified the interpretation?

Use preview + structured interpretation.

Do not make users open a separate “AI analysis” screen to understand the document.

---

# 25. Client Privacy Memory

Client Memory should feel like the persistent factual context of the client, not a knowledge graph UI.

Suggested client overview:

### What we know
High-confidence accepted facts.

### Current concerns
Material gaps, contradictions or unresolved matters.

### Processing
Important processing activities and recent changes.

### Decisions
Recent or consequential professional decisions.

### Actions
Open work requiring follow-through.

### Privexa found
A small review queue of candidate facts extracted from new material.

Once accepted, items become ordinary memory.

---

# 26. Home and portfolio experience

The home screen should not be a wall of KPIs.

Recommended information hierarchy:

1. **Needs my judgement**
2. **What changed**
3. **Being handled for you**
4. **Upcoming deadlines / commitments**
5. **Practice snapshot**

Example:

```text
Good morning

5 things need your judgement
1. Apollo Finance — DPIA residual risk
2. Northstar Health — RoPA contradiction
3. ...

What changed
1 material client change since yesterday

Being handled for you
7 routine follow-ups in progress
```

Metrics appear after work, not before it.

---

# 27. Metrics and charts

Privexa should use charts sparingly.

Prefer deterministic metrics that describe meaningful professional outcomes:

- active clients;
- processing activities maintained;
- DPIAs prepared;
- Opinions issued;
- Actions progressed;
- matters awaiting judgement;
- evidence freshness;
- autonomous follow-through;
- client capacity.

Avoid:

- token counts;
- AI call counts;
- arbitrary productivity scores;
- “privacy maturity 83%” without rigorous methodology.

Charts should be:

- flat;
- labelled directly where possible;
- lightly gridded;
- neutral-first with accent emphasis;
- limited to 1–2 analytical questions per view.

---

# 28. Motion

Motion should communicate continuity and state.

Recommended timing:

| Interaction | Duration |
|---|---:|
| Micro hover/focus | 100–140 ms |
| Dropdown/popover | 140–180 ms |
| Panel/sheet | 180–240 ms |
| Page-level transition | 200–280 ms |
| AI result reveal | progressive, content-dependent |

Use ease-out for entering, ease-in for exiting.

Avoid:

- elastic/springy motion for professional workflows;
- celebratory confetti;
- pulsing AI elements;
- unnecessary loading animations.

AI processing states should be calm:

`Reading evidence…`
`Checking affected processing activities…`
`Preparing 3 review items…`

Where possible, show meaningful stage progress rather than a generic spinner.

---

# 29. Empty states

Empty states should be useful, not cute.

Good:

> **No processing activities yet**  
> Add one manually or let Privexa discover candidate activities from existing evidence.

Actions:

**Add activity**  
Discover from evidence

Avoid mascots, illustrations or jokes in core professional workflows.

---

# 30. Loading, uncertainty and errors

## 30.1 Loading

Use skeletons for known layouts.

For AI work, state the operation:

- Reading 4 evidence items
- Checking applicable obligations
- Comparing with the current RoPA

## 30.2 Uncertainty

Never hide uncertainty behind confident copy.

Preferred:

> **2 facts are still missing before this is ready for a decision.**

Not:

> “Privexa is 76% confident.”

Confidence percentages should only be shown if they have a real calibrated interpretation.

## 30.3 Errors

Use precise, recoverable language.

Good:

> **The document was stored, but text extraction failed.**  
> You can still open the original file or retry extraction.

Avoid:

> Something went wrong.

---

# 31. Notifications and attention design

Privexa should be conservative with notifications.

Use attention tiers:

1. **Judgement required** — a human decision is blocked or needed.
2. **Time-sensitive** — deterministic deadline or escalation.
3. **Review suggested** — evidence/RoPA change merits review.
4. **FYI** — completed routine activity.

Do not turn every AI finding into a badge.

Badge counts should represent actionable items, not general activity.

# 32. Content and voice

## 32.1 Voice

Privexa speaks like a highly competent professional assistant:

- concise;
- neutral;
- specific;
- evidence-aware;
- calm;
- non-theatrical.

It does not:

- hype;
- flatter;
- dramatize;
- anthropomorphize itself excessively;
- make autonomous legal claims;
- imply certainty without support.

## 32.2 Preferred vocabulary

Prefer:

- **prepare**
- **review**
- **evidence**
- **source**
- **processing activity**
- **affected**
- **assumption**
- **decision**
- **condition**
- **requires judgement**
- **suggested update**
- **current**
- **not documented**
- **source-backed**

Avoid:

- magic;
- genius;
- autopilot;
- compliance solved;
- fully automatic;
- AI-powered everywhere;
- risk-free;
- guaranteed compliant.

## 32.3 Microcopy examples

Good:

> Privexa found 6 possible processing activities in the documents you uploaded.

Good:

> 3 items need your review.

Good:

> This recommendation uses 2 client facts, 4 evidence items and 1 firm position.

Good:

> Retention is not documented.

Better than:

> Retention information is missing!!!

# 33. Accessibility

The premium feel depends on accessibility quality.

Minimum expectations:

- meet WCAG 2.2 AA for production UI;
- 44 px touch targets where practical;
- clear keyboard focus;
- complete keyboard operation for tables and editors;
- no state conveyed through color only;
- descriptive labels for icons;
- reduced-motion support;
- logical heading hierarchy;
- form errors connected to fields;
- sufficient text/background contrast;
- readable zoom at 200%;
- focus returned correctly after dialogs/sheets.

Accessibility QA should be part of component acceptance, not a later retrofit.

# 35. Client portal

The client-facing experience should be even simpler than the professional app.

A client should see:

> **Your privacy team needs 3 things from you.**

Then ordinary-language tasks.

Examples:

- Confirm how long support recordings are retained.
- Upload the current payroll vendor agreement.
- Answer 4 questions about the new AI support project.

Avoid exposing:

- internal agent names;
- internal risk scoring;
- RoPA architecture;
- legal reasoning graphs;
- system prompts;
- model/provider details.

The professional owns the privacy machinery. The client receives a clear collaboration experience.

White-labeling should change identity, not product integrity.

# 42. Do / do not

## Do

- make whitespace part of the information architecture;
- keep active client context visible;
- use typography to create hierarchy;
- make AI sources inspectable;
- keep AI suggestions reversible;
- show missing information explicitly;
- give consequential decisions dedicated space;
- use status words in addition to color;
- design RoPA as a living map;
- design DPIA as reasoning over known facts;
- keep the product fast with keyboard-friendly interaction;
- make auditability available without making every screen look like an audit log.

## Do not

- build a dense dashboard as the default home;
- use a shield/lock visual theme;
- put AI in a permanent chat sidebar everywhere;
- use gradients as the main product aesthetic;
- preselect human decisions;
- turn every domain relationship into a visible graph;
- lead with compliance scores;
- expose agent names to end users;
- make users classify every uploaded file manually;
- create a separate visual system for RoPA, DPIA, Evidence and Decisions;
- use “magic” or autonomous-compliance language;
- overuse badges, borders, pills or icons.

The product should have a few moments that make the brand memorable through utility.

Calm review, one fact at a time, then accepted facts become ordinary Client Memory.

Review candidate activities without forcing spreadsheet entry.

- Ask client

## 43.5 Continuous RoPA

Show current → proposed, sources, and explicit review.

A useful professional opening screen, not a metric dashboard.

# 44. Brand QA checklist

Before a new screen is approved, ask:

- [ ] Is the active client/firm context clear where required?
- [ ] Is the primary action obvious?
- [ ] Can any card, border, badge, icon or color be removed?
- [ ] Is AI-prepared content distinguishable from approved content?
- [ ] Are material sources inspectable?
- [ ] Is uncertainty explicit?
- [ ] Is professional judgement visually separated from AI recommendation?
- [ ] Is any important state encoded only by color?
- [ ] Does the screen use sentence case?
- [ ] Is regulatory jargon exposed only to the users who need it?
- [ ] Does the screen avoid arbitrary scores?
- [ ] Does the screen avoid generic “AI magic” language?
- [ ] Can a keyboard user complete the workflow?
- [ ] Does the responsive behavior preserve the decision, not merely the layout?
- [ ] Does the interface feel calm at realistic data density?
- [ ] Does the experience still make sense if AI is temporarily unavailable?

# 45. Brand acceptance test

Privexa is on-brand when a user can say:

> “It feels serious without feeling bureaucratic.”

> “I can see what the AI did without having to understand the AI stack.”

> “The product knows a lot, but it only shows me what matters now.”

> “I always know which client I am working in.”

> “The evidence is close to the judgement.”

> “The system prepares; I decide.”

> “The RoPA feels alive rather than annual.”

> “The DPIA starts from what we already know.”

If the product instead feels like an analytics dashboard, a security console, a chatbot, or an online compliance spreadsheet, the brand direction has drifted.
